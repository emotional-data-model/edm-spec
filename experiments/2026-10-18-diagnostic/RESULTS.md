## 0. What was done

For each of the six core slots (v0.9 names; v0.8.3 `core.anchor`, `spark`, `wound`, `fuel`, `bridge`, `echo`) and each of the 150 run-1 texts, the four extractors' **modal answers** were taken exactly as `score.py` takes them for the cross-model statistic: per extractor, the judge-equivalence class (from the run-1 Opus 5.5 judge pass, null included) that most of its five repeats fall in, represented by the most frequent wording in that class. Every text × slot whose four modal classes are not all the same is a **disagreement item**. One Opus 5.5 call per item classified the disagreement as (a) multiple valid referents in the text, (b) one or more extractors wrong (listing which), (c) granularity only (same referent, different specificity), or (d) null vs populated (with which side the text supports). Precedence fixed in the prompt: (b) over (a) and (c); (d) only when the populated answers are mutually equivalent, otherwise the populated disagreement is classified and `null_present` is set. Extractors were shown to the judge as anonymised labels A–D in a per-item shuffled order (`label_of` in each record); the labels are mapped back here. Prompts: `prompts/diagnostic_system.txt`, `prompts/diagnostic_user.txt`; the slot definitions shown to the judge are the run-1 extraction-prompt blocks verbatim (`items.json` → `definitions`).

**Ground truth.** The founder instruction asked for the authored value from the synthetic design matrix to be included where one exists. The design matrix (`experiments/2026-10-17/corpus/synthetic_design_matrix.json`) and the authored texts (`synthetic_texts_months_*.json` → `spans`) carry ground truth for `meaning`, `self_position`, `bodily_sensation`, `attribution`, `function`, `turning_point` and `trait` only; **no authored value exists for any of the six slots** (as `experiment-plan-run2.md` §8 already states), so none was passed and the synthetic arm is judged against the text alone, like the other two arms.

## 4. Recommendation per slot

Written by the delegated session on 2026-10-18 from the tables above; one paragraph per slot, each ending in one of the three options the founder instruction named (single string with a selection rule / short list / mark interpretive). These are recommendations for the run-2 plan (`experiment-plan-run2.md`, amended the same day, still not frozen), not rulings: R2 holds, nothing in `schema/` or `examples/` changes, and run 2 decides. The figures are run-1 statistics under the one 47-field prompt (R3); the diagnostic says *why* the four extractors disagreed under that prompt, which is the information a prompt test needs.

**`narrative.subject` (v0.8.3 `anchor`) — keep as a single string with a selection rule.** 122 of 150 texts are disagreement items, and in 48 % of them the judge found more than one valid referent (27 of those 58 list three or more: a conversation session or a Pepys day has several candidate centres), in 28 % the same referent at different specificity, and in only 13 % an extractor that was wrong (wrong-rate 0.01–0.07 per extractor). The field is not misread; it is under-selected. A list would record the ambiguity rather than resolve it and would not match the field's own definition ("central person, object, or theme"). The Arm B referent rule ("the one the text returns to most") addresses the 48 %; a specificity clause ("the most specific noun phrase the text itself uses that fits in 1–5 words") addresses the 28 %. Both are now in the Arm B block.

**`narrative.trigger` (v0.8.3 `spark`) — keep as a single string with a selection rule, and an explicit exclusion.** This is the slot with the highest extractor-wrong share (28 %; 36 % on Pepys) and the highest wrong-rates (Sonnet 0.12, Kimi 0.14 on (b) alone): extractors name the topic or the subject ("seeking writing prompts") or a later episode instead of the initiating moment, and the (d) items split evenly between null-wrong and populated-wrong (8 / 8), i.e. the extractors also disagree on whether a diffuse mood has a trigger at all. The definition, not the field, is doing this. Arm B now says "the moment, event or perception the text presents as starting the response it dwells on most; not the topic and not the subject", and "null when the text states a feeling without an initiating event". The 42 % multiple-referent items are two-episode texts (35 of 44 list exactly two) and fall to the same selection rule.

**`narrative.harm` (v0.8.3 `wound`) — keep as a single string with a selection rule and a null threshold.** 97 items; (a) 37 %, (c) 28 %, (d) 26 %, (b) 9 %. On the conversational arm two-thirds of the disagreements are null-vs-populated, and across arms the text supported the populated answer in 17 of 25 (d) items: extractors under-populate a stated worry rather than invent a wound. The granularity items are the text's own word against a paraphrase ("self-doubt" vs "doubt about personal patience"). Arm B now states the threshold ("a stated worry, fear, loss or vulnerability counts; one you would have to infer does not; null otherwise") and the wording rule ("the text's own word where it has one"). Not a list: when populated, the extractors mostly agree on the referent.

**`narrative.drive` (v0.8.3 `fuel`) — mark interpretive unless Arm B overturns it.** The most contested slot (126 of 150 texts) and the most mixed: (a) 33 %, (d) 27 % (29 of 34 null-wrong), (c) 23 %, (b) 17 %, and a null answer sits beside the populated disagreement in a further 30 items. "What energized or motivated the experience" is read as a motive, an emotion, a person or a topic by different extractors on the same text, and the three classes that are not extractor error together cover 83 % of the items, so tightening the definition alone is not expected to make the answers comparable. Arm B carries a selection rule and a null rule so that run 2 gives the field its chance; the pre-registered expectation is that it lands interpretive (repeatable within an extractor, not comparable across), in which case it keeps `x_comparability: "interpretive"` and no cross-system comparison is claimed for it. A list is not recommended: it would not touch the null disagreement.

**`narrative.integration` (v0.8.3 `bridge`) — keep as a single string with a null rule.** Only 59 of 150 texts are disagreement items, because the field is null two-thirds of the time and the extractors agree on that. Where they disagree it is mostly whether to populate at all ((d) 36 %; 18 of 21 null-wrong; 7 of 8 Pepys items), and when they populate they name the same shift at different specificity or two valid shifts (25 % + 29 %); extractor error is 10 %. The run-1 pass was null-driven (R4) and this diagnostic says the populated answers are not the problem; the under-population is. Arm B keeps "Null if the text draws none" and adds what counts as drawing one ("a comparison of then and now, a lesson stated, a change of mind the text names"). Not interpretive, not a list.

**`salience.retrieval_cues` (v0.8.3 `echo`) — change to a short, ordered list with a cap of three and a first-element comparison rule.** 137 of 150 texts disagree, and the diagnostic says why: in 53 % of items the judge listed several valid cues (65 of 73 such items list four or more), in 34 % one extractor returned an empty list where the text names a cue (43 of 47 (d) items null-wrong; `gpt-6.1-sol` alone is wrong in 44 of 137 items because it leaves the list empty), and extractor error is 4 %. The texts offer many true answers, and the run-1 scoring compared whole lists, so every selection difference counted as disagreement. The recommendation is the list the v0.9 draft already has, but bounded and ordered: at most three cues, the first being the one the text returns to most, scored by the first element (judge rule 5 already says so) and with an explicit rule that an empty list means the text names nothing that recurs. Run 2 tests this against the v0.8.3 single string `echo` (Arm A) on the same texts; if the single string passes and the list does not, the v0.8.3 shape returns. The empty-list behaviour of one extractor is reported as an extractor effect, not scored as a field property.

**Across slots.** Extractor error accounts for 13 % of the 645 disagreements; multiple valid referents (42 %), granularity (20 %) and null-vs-populated (24 %) account for the rest. The wrong-rates are ordered Sonnet 0.09 < Haiku 0.12 < gpt-6.1-sol 0.15 < Kimi 0.22 on (b)+(d), with the two cheaper voting extractors contributing most of the null-side errors; this does not change R9 (Kimi votes) but is recorded in the run-2 plan as a caveat on the three-voter rule. The synthetic arm is not easier on these slots (215 items against 203 conversational and 227 Pepys), which is consistent with there being no authored ground truth for them: the texts were written to state the seven flagged elements, not to have one anchor, spark, wound, fuel, bridge or echo.
