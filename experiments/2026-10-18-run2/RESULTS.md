## 7. Ruling drafts per slot (for the founder)

Drafts for the founder, written by the delegated session on 2026-10-18 from §1–§5 above. Each is one of the three options the founder instruction named — **adopt Arm A** (v0.8.3 text under the v0.9 name), **adopt Arm B rules** (the question-framed definition with selection / null rules), or **mark interpretive** — plus what the frozen §7 table says about profile membership. They are drafts: R2 holds, nothing in `schema/` or `examples/` changes, the founder rules. Figures are pooled over 150 texts under the frozen three-voter rule (Sonnet 5.5, gpt-6.1-sol, Kimi K2.6; repeat ≥ 0.80, cross ≥ 0.60); the two-voter sensitivity (Sonnet + GPT) is cited where it changes the reading. The control held (`summary` 0.96 / 0.98), so the A / B contrast is a slot effect.

**`narrative.harm` (`wound`) — adopt Arm B rules; keep in Essential as a single string.** Arm B passes (repeat 0.90 / 0.85 / 0.82, cross 0.64, populated-only 0.61); Arm A is borderline-cut only because Kimi repeats at 0.79, and passes under the two-voter sensitivity (cross 0.66). The frozen table reads this as "v0.8.3 text at fault", which overstates it: both texts name the same wound on two-thirds of texts (cross-arm equivalence 0.62–0.66) and the B rule's gain is that Kimi now repeats it. The null threshold ("a stated worry, fear, loss or vulnerability counts") did not change the null rate (0.35 → 0.33), so it cost nothing and bought a stable floor. Per arm: conversational passes under both texts (0.82 / 0.78), synthetic and Pepys do not pass under either. The prediction (pass, null falls) held. Draft ruling: `harm` keeps the Arm B definition and shape; Essential; no `x_comparability` annotation.

**`narrative.trigger` (`spark`) — adopt Arm B rules and mark interpretive.** Arm B is interpretive (all three voters repeat ≥ 0.80, cross 0.56, populated-only 0.61, borderline); Arm A is borderline-cut (Kimi 0.74) and interpretive under the two-voter sensitivity (cross 0.51). The topic / subject exclusion and the null clause moved the null rate from 0.07 to 0.15 and raised cross agreement from 0.53 to 0.56; on synthetic text both arms pass (0.70 / 0.67), on Pepys neither. The two texts name the same trigger on 54–69 % of texts, so the renaming does change what is extracted on a third of them. The prediction (pass or interpretive) held. Draft ruling: `trigger` keeps the Arm B definition, stays in Essential as a single string, carries `x_comparability: "interpretive"` (repeatable within an extractor, cross-model agreement just below the bar), and no cross-system comparison is claimed for it until a run passes it.

**`narrative.subject` (`anchor`) — adopt Arm A text and mark interpretive; membership is the founder's call.** Neither arm passes: both are borderline-cut (Kimi 0.76 / 0.72) and both are interpretive under the two-voter sensitivity (Sonnet 0.91 / 0.94, GPT 0.85 / 0.84; cross2 0.58 / 0.54). The Arm B selection rule made agreement worse, not better (cross 0.53 → 0.41; synthetic: A passes at 0.77, B is interpretive at 0.58), and the two texts agree with each other on only 36–79 % of texts: "the one the text returns to most" sends the extractors to different centres, which is what the diagnostic's 48 % multiple-valid-referent share predicted a rule would have to overcome and this rule did not. The prediction (pass) did not hold. Under the frozen table (neither passes) the pre-registered recommendation is to move the slot out of Essential; the sensitivity says the slot is interpretive rather than unrepeatable. Draft ruling: `subject` keeps the v0.8.3 wording under the v0.9 name, the Arm B rule is withdrawn for it, `x_comparability: "interpretive"`; whether it stays in Essential is the R2 decision, with the pre-registered recommendation (move to Extended) stated and the two-voter reading beside it.

**`narrative.integration` (`bridge`) — adopt Arm A text; keep in Essential as a single string.** Arm A is borderline-cut on Kimi alone (0.75) and passes under the two-voter sensitivity (cross 0.71; three-voter cross 0.67, populated-only 0.60, both above the bar); Arm B is borderline-cut on GPT (0.79) and cut under the sensitivity. The slot is comparable whenever it is repeated; what fails is one voter's repeatability, a different one in each arm. The Arm B null clause did nothing (null 0.48 → 0.48), and the two texts name the same shift on 58–69 % of texts. The prediction (pass, null falls) did not hold. Under the frozen table neither passes and the pre-registered recommendation is to move it; the figures say the v0.8.3 wording is the better text and the field is one voter short of a pass. Draft ruling: `integration` keeps the v0.8.3 wording under the v0.9 name, stays in Essential, carries `x_comparability: "null-driven"` lifted to no annotation only if a later run passes it with all three voters; the Arm B definition is withdrawn for it.

**`narrative.drive` (`fuel`) — mark interpretive and move out of Essential (pre-registered recommendation).** Arm A is borderline-cut (Kimi 0.68; cross 0.39) and interpretive under the sensitivity (cross2 0.54); Arm B is cut (GPT 0.78, Kimi 0.70; cross 0.46) under both rules. Cross-arm equivalence 0.40–0.53: the two definitions do not even ask the same question of the same text. The prediction (interpretive) did not hold because B fell to cut, but the direction it predicted — not comparable across models under either text — is what the run shows; the diagnostic's reading (a motive, an emotion, a person or a topic, depending on the extractor) stands. Draft ruling: `drive` leaves Essential for Extended, keeps the v0.8.3 wording under the v0.9 name, carries `x_comparability: "interpretive"`; migration preserves every v0.8.3 `fuel` value.

**`salience.retrieval_cues` (`echo`) — neither shape replicates; move out of Essential (pre-registered recommendation), Arm A single string preferred.** Arm A (single string `echo`) is borderline-cut (Kimi 0.68; Sonnet 0.85, GPT 0.80) with cross 0.40; Arm B (capped ordered list, first-element judging) is cut (GPT 0.77, Kimi 0.70) with cross 0.40. The cap removed the empty lists (null 0.31 → 0.05 across five extractors; `gpt-6.1-sol` 0.49 → 0.05) without raising agreement, and the two shapes name the same first cue on only 24–35 % of texts. The prediction (B passes where the run-1 list did not) did not hold. The diagnostic's finding — the texts offer four or more valid cues on most disagreement items — is confirmed from the other side: a rule that asks for "the one the text returns to most" does not make four extractors pick the same one. Draft ruling: `retrieval_cues` leaves Essential for Extended as a short list (the v0.9 shape, since the single string did no better), carries `x_comparability: "extractor-bound"`; `echo` values migrate as the first element.

**Across slots.** Two of six frozen predictions held (`trigger`, `harm`). Kimi K2.6 is the voter below the repeatability bar in 8 of the 12 slot × arm cells (0.68–0.80), gpt-6.1-sol in 3 Arm B cells (0.77–0.79); the two-voter sensitivity turns two Arm A borderline-cuts (`harm`, `integration`) into passes and nothing else, so Kimi alone sank exactly those two cells and the rest fail on cross-model agreement. R9 stands as frozen; the founder may wish to weigh whether a voter that repeats at 0.68–0.80 on 1–5-word slots should carry a veto in run 3. The synthetic arm passes more cells than the other two (6 of 12 non-control cells pass or are interpretive against 4 conversational and 0 Pepys), as in run 1. Task (a) on the rebuilt LoCoMo ground truth passes for all three voters under every policy (`S_all` +0.25 / +0.12 / +0.38 over recency 0.25, random floor 0.30), which closes R8's open conversational question on these two conversations. The reported arms: Haiku 5.5 repeats at 0.55–0.84 and agrees with the voting majority on 55–79 % of texts; `gpt-5.4-mini` repeats at 0.52–0.84 and agrees on 28–66 %, so neither is a substitute for a voter on these slots.
